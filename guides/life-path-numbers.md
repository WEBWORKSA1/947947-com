Your life path number comes from your date of birth. In numerology it describes the overall direction of a life: the strengths you lean on, the lessons that keep coming back and the kind of work and relationships that tend to suit you. Like all numerology, it is a tradition for reflection rather than a scientific measurement.

## How to calculate it

The most widely taught method reduces each part of the date separately, then adds them.

1. **Month.** Reduce the month to one digit, unless it is 11. November (11) stays 11.
2. **Day.** Reduce the day, keeping 11 and 22. The 29th becomes 2 + 9 = 11.
3. **Year.** Add the digits of the year and reduce. 1987 → 1 + 9 + 8 + 7 = 25 → 7.
4. **Add the three results and reduce again,** keeping 11, 22 and 33. 11 + 11 + 7 = 29 → 2 + 9 = 11.

So someone born on 29 November 1987 has life path 11. The [life path calculator]({{ r }}tools/life-path-calculator/) does this for you and shows every step.

## Why two methods can disagree

Some numerologists simply add every digit of the full date: 2 + 9 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 9 + 8 + 7 = 38 → 11. Here the answers match, but for some dates the shortcut skips past a master number that the month-day-year method would find. Neither method is "scientific"; we use month-day-year because it is the most common and it treats master numbers consistently.

## Master numbers

11, 22 and 33 are called master numbers. They are read as intensified versions of 2, 4 and 6: more sensitive, more ambitious or more devoted, with more pressure attached. If you are an 11, read the meaning of 2 as well, because many numerologists see the master number as a potential you grow into.

## Life path compatibility

Numerologists often group the numbers into three families whose priorities line up: 1, 5 and 7 (independent thinkers), 2, 4 and 8 (builders and organisers), and 3, 6 and 9 (creative carers). Same-family pairs are called natural matches. Compare two dates with the [compatibility calculator]({{ r }}tools/compatibility-calculator/).
