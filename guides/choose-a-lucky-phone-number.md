A phone number is one of the few numbers you share every day. Across much of Asia, people pay real money for "good" numbers, and many businesses choose memorable ones to be easier to call back. Whether you believe in lucky numbers or not, a little thought costs nothing.

## Three ways to judge a number

### 1. Numerology

Add every digit and reduce to a single digit or a master number. That is the number's root. Numerologists favour a root that matches or harmonises with your [life path number]({{ r }}tools/life-path-calculator/). The traditional families are 1, 5 and 7; 2, 4 and 8; and 3, 6 and 9. Some practitioners give extra weight to the last four digits.

### 2. Chinese, Japanese and Korean digit beliefs

- **Look for** 8 (prosper), 6 (smooth) and 9 (long-lasting).
- **Avoid** 4, which sounds like death in Chinese, Japanese and Korean, and 9 in Japanese contexts.
- **Watch for hidden codes.** 168 (fortune all the way) and 518 (I will prosper) are welcome; 748 (go die) and 250 (fool) are not. See our list of [Chinese number slang]({{ r }}guides/chinese-lucky-numbers/).

### 3. Memorability

Repeats (777), pairs (8866), sequences (1234) and mirrors (1221) are easier to remember and to say. For businesses in North America, a number that spells a word on the keypad, such as 1-800 numbers, is the classic choice.

## How to actually get the number you want

- **Ask your mobile operator.** Many operators let you pick from available numbers when you sign up or for a small fee, and some sell "golden" or premium numbers.
- **Keep your number when you switch.** Number portability lets you move an existing number between operators in most countries.
- **Business numbers.** Virtual phone providers sell local and toll-free numbers and often let you search for patterns or words.
- **Avoid unofficial resellers** that ask for payment before showing proof of ownership or a transfer process.

## A sensible approach

Choose a number you can remember and that does not offend the people who will call it. If you care about numerology, aim for a harmonious root.
