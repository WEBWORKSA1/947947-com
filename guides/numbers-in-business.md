Numbers carry meaning before anyone reads a word of your marketing. For brands selling into Greater China, Southeast Asia, Japan, Korea or India, a few numeric choices can signal care, or carelessness. The good news: the fixes are cheap if you make them before printing.

## Prices

- **Chinese markets** favour 8 (prosper) and 6 (smooth) and avoid 4 (death). A price ending in 88 reads as generous; one ending in 44 can read as unlucky.
- **Western markets** are used to "charm" prices ending in 9 or 99, which make a price feel lower.
- **Japan** avoids 4 and 9 in gift contexts, so gift sets of four items are rare.

## Product and model numbers

Some technology brands have skipped the number 4 in product lines because of East Asian tetraphobia; the phone maker OnePlus, for example, went from its 3 series straight to the OnePlus 5. If your products are numbered, check the sequence for 4s, 13s and unfortunate codes before launch.

## Phone numbers and addresses

A hotline full of 4s, or one hiding the code 748 (go die in Chinese slang), sends the wrong signal. Buildings across Asia skip fourth floors, and many Western ones skip the 13th. Showrooms, hotels and serviced offices often choose unit numbers with 8s. Score any number with our [phone number checker]({{ r }}tools/phone-number-numerology/).

## Dates

- **Auspicious dates** with 8, 6 or 9 are popular for openings and weddings in Chinese communities; 8 August is a favourite.
- **The seventh lunar month,** ghost month, is avoided for launches and moves by many Chinese families.
- **India:** many businesses ask an astrologer for an auspicious time (*muhurat*) to open a shop or sign a deal.
- **The West:** Friday the 13th is still avoided by some for weddings and launches.

## Names

Name numerology is popular in India and with some founders worldwide. If it matters to you or your partners, check the name's [expression number]({{ r }}tools/name-numerology-calculator/) alongside the trademark search.

## A pre-launch checklist

1. List every customer-facing number: prices, phone lines, model numbers, addresses, dates.
2. Mark any with 4 (East Asia), 13 (West) or 17 (Italy) and any slang codes.
3. Prefer endings in 8 or 6 for Chinese audiences, if the price works.
4. Check dates against ghost month and local holidays.
5. Write down why you chose each number, so your team can explain it.
